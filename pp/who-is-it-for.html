Who is it for?

Lone Workers

A lone worker is an employee who performs tasks or work that is carried out in isolation from other workers without close or direct supervision.

With over 8 million lone workers in the UK, it is more important than ever that employers establish a healthy and safe working environment for their employees. It may often be safe for workers to work alone however, the law requires employers to take risks into consideration before people are allowed to do so.

Pick Protection can help you adhere to the following legislation, which has been put in place to protect employees at work and is essential to ensure the health, safety and welfare of lone workers.

Protecting Lone Workers

Health And Safety At Work Act 1974

The Health and Safety at Work Act 1974 (also referred to as HSWA, the 1974 Act, the HSW or HASAWA) is the primary piece of UK health and safety legislation. It requires employers to ‘ensure, so far as is reasonably practicable, the health, safety and welfare at work’ of all of their employees.

The main requirements of this Act are:
  • The safe operation and maintenance of the working environment, plant and systems
  • Maintenance of safe access and egress to the workplace
  • Safe use, handling and storage of dangerous substances
  • Adequate training of staff to ensure health and safety
  • Adequate welfare provisions for staff at work
